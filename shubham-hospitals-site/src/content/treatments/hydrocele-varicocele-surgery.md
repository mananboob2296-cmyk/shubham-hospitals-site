## What it is

Two different conditions are often lumped together because both cause a swelling or heaviness in the scrotum, and both are treated by the same surgical team.

A **hydrocele** is a collection of clear fluid in the thin sac that surrounds the testis. The scrotum looks swollen, usually on one side, and feels smooth and tense, but it is typically painless. A **varicocele** is a different problem: the veins that drain the testis become dilated and twisted, a little like varicose veins in the leg. It is far commoner on the left side and is often described as feeling like a bag of worms.

Surgery for a hydrocele removes or reorganises the sac so the fluid cannot collect again. **Varicocele surgery (varicocelectomy)** ties off the abnormal veins so that blood no longer pools around the testis.

## Who needs it

Not every swelling needs an operation, and I would rather tell you that plainly. These are the situations where I do recommend one.

**Hydrocele:**

- **The swelling is large or growing** and has become uncomfortable, heavy or embarrassing.
- **It interferes with walking, sitting or intimacy.**
- **It has lasted beyond infancy.** In babies we usually wait, because many settle by themselves.
- **Repeated draining has not worked**, and the fluid keeps coming back.

**Varicocele:**

- **Pain or a dragging ache** that does not settle with simple measures.
- **The testis on that side is smaller** than the other in an adolescent or young man.
- **Fertility concerns** - a couple has not conceived, the semen analysis is abnormal and a varicocele can be felt on examination.

> **"I have no pain. Should I still get it checked?"**
> Yes, once. A painless swelling is exactly the kind people ignore for years. A quick examination and an ultrasound tell us what it is, and in most cases the answer is reassuring. What I never want is a hard, painless lump in the testis itself being mistaken for a hydrocele - that needs prompt attention.

## How it is done

Before surgery, an ultrasound of the scrotum confirms the diagnosis and rules out anything else. For a varicocele being treated for fertility, a recent semen analysis is also needed.

**Hydrocele surgery.** A small cut is made on the scrotum, the sac is opened and the fluid drained. The sac is then either turned inside out and stitched behind the testis (eversion) or trimmed and plicated so it cannot refill. A small drain or a firm dressing may be used. In children the approach is usually through a small groin incision, because the sac often connects to the abdomen.

**Varicocele surgery.** The abnormal veins are identified and tied off, taking care to preserve the testicular artery and the lymphatic channels. Depending on your anatomy, the surgeon may work through a small incision in the groin or just below it, often with a microscope or magnifying loupes to separate the artery from the veins. In some centres the same result is achieved by blocking the veins through a catheter, which is done by a radiologist; we will tell you if that suits you better.

When a man has both a hydrocele and a varicocele on the same side, they can often be dealt with in a single sitting.

## Anaesthesia, duration and hospital stay

Both operations are done under **spinal anaesthesia** or a short **general anaesthetic**; you feel nothing and wake up comfortable. A hydrocele operation takes **about 30 to 45 minutes**, and a varicocele repair **about 45 to 60 minutes**, a little longer if both are done together.

These are usually **day-care or overnight stay** procedures. You walk within a few hours, eat the same day and go home that evening or the next morning.

## Recovery

**Day 1.** Expect a dull ache, some swelling and a snug dressing. Wear the scrotal support provided, keep the area clean and dry, and take the pain medicines as advised. Walk around the house.

**The first week.** Swelling and bruising are normal, and the scrotum can look alarmingly purple or larger than before for several days. It settles. Rest with the scrotum supported, avoid prolonged standing and keep the wound dry. Desk work is possible from about day 3 to 7.

**Weeks 2 to 4.** The wound heals and the stitches, which dissolve, are no longer felt. Increase walking. Avoid heavy lifting, cycling, running and the gym until you are comfortable and we have reviewed you.

**By 6 weeks.** Most men are back to full activity. A hydrocele can leave some firmness or thickening for a couple of months, which is normal. If the operation was for fertility, a repeat semen analysis is generally done after about three months, because sperm take that long to renew.

> **"The swelling got bigger after the operation. Has it failed?"**
> Almost certainly not. Some swelling and bruising in the first week is part of healing, especially after a hydrocele operation. Call us if the swelling is increasing rapidly, if there is fever, or if the pain is getting worse rather than better.

## Risks and complications

Both are straightforward operations, but you should know what can happen.

- **Swelling, bruising and a haematoma** (collection of blood) in the scrotum.
- **Wound infection**, usually mild.
- **Recurrence** of the hydrocele or varicocele.
- **A hydrocele forming after varicocele surgery**, because lymph drainage is affected. This is why the lymphatics are protected.
- **Injury to the testicular artery**, which can in rare cases reduce the blood supply and shrink the testis.
- **Persistent pain or numbness** near the scar, usually temporary.
- **Rarely, injury to the cord structures** (the tube that carries sperm).
- **The usual risks of anaesthesia**, which the anaesthetist will go through with you.

Varicocele repair does not guarantee a better semen report or a pregnancy; I would rather you hear that from me now.

## Alternatives

**For a hydrocele:**

- **Watchful waiting** if it is small and not troubling you, and in infants.
- **Aspiration** (draining the fluid with a needle), sometimes followed by an injection to scar the sac. It gives temporary relief, but the fluid commonly returns, and there is a small risk of infection or bleeding. I generally reserve it for men who cannot have an operation.

**For a varicocele:**

- **Observation** with periodic checks if there is no pain, no size difference and no fertility problem.
- **Supportive underwear and pain relief** for mild discomfort.
- **Embolisation** - blocking the veins through a catheter, done by a radiologist.
- **Assisted conception** such as [ICSI](/treatments/icsi/), or [surgical sperm retrieval](/treatments/surgical-sperm-retrieval/) where needed, when fertility is the main problem and repair is not enough or not advisable.

## Preparing for your surgery

- **Bring your records** - previous scans, semen analysis reports, prescriptions and your list of medicines.
- **Get the scrotal ultrasound done** before the consultation if you have not already.
- **Tell us about blood thinners**, aspirin, diabetes or blood pressure medicines; never stop a heart medicine on your own.
- **Fasting.** Nothing to eat for six hours and nothing to drink for two hours before surgery, or as the anaesthetist instructs.
- **Shave or trim** the area as advised on the day, and wear clean, snug briefs.
- **Arrange a scrotal support** (we can advise which) and a day or two of rest at home.
- **If this is for fertility,** come with your wife if you can, and bring her reports; the decision is a joint one.
