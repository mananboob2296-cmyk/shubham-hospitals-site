## What it is

Labiaplasty, also called labia reduction surgery, is a small operation to reduce or reshape the labia minora, the inner lips of the vulva. Occasionally the outer lips (labia majora) or the fold of skin over the clitoris are adjusted at the same time. The aim is modest and practical: to remove the tissue that is rubbing, pulling or getting in the way, and to leave a natural-looking result.

It is one of the procedures offered by our [cosmetic and aesthetic gynaecology unit](/treatments/cosmetic-aesthetic-gynecology/). You can also [see all the procedures we perform](/procedures/).

## Who needs it

Let me begin with something I say in almost every consultation: there is no single "normal" for labia. Inner lips that show beyond the outer lips, or a longer lip on one side, are ordinary variations and not a medical problem.

Labiaplasty is worth considering when the labia cause trouble that keeps coming back:

- rubbing, chafing or soreness in fitted clothes, or while walking, cycling or exercising;
- the labia being pulled or pinched during intercourse;
- difficulty keeping the area clean, or repeated irritation;
- a marked difference between the two sides that bothers you;
- lasting self-consciousness about the appearance, enough to affect your confidence or your intimate life.

It is not suitable for girls under 18, because the labia are still developing. We also do not operate during pregnancy, when there is an untreated infection or skin condition of the vulva, or when the result a woman hopes for is something surgery cannot realistically give.

> **"I have never told anyone about this. Is it silly to come to a doctor for it?"**
> Not at all. Women live with this discomfort for years because it feels too awkward to mention. The consultation is private and unhurried. I will examine you, tell you honestly whether what you have is within the usual range, and explain every option. Quite often, reassurance and a few simple measures are all that is needed, and nobody will push you towards an operation.

## How it is done

Before anything else we talk through what bothers you and what you would like to change, and I mark the planned result with you so there are no surprises.

There are two common techniques. In the **trim (edge) technique**, the excess tissue along the free edge of the labia is removed and the edge is closed with fine stitches. In the **wedge technique**, a V-shaped piece is taken from the thickest part and the two edges are brought together, which keeps the natural border of the labia. Which one suits you depends on the shape and thickness of your own tissue, and I will explain why I am recommending one over the other.

The tissue is removed carefully, bleeding points are sealed, and the wound is closed with fine dissolving stitches that do not need to be taken out. If the fold over the clitoris is bulky, it may be reduced slightly in the same sitting, but only if you and I agreed on this beforehand. The surgery stays well clear of the clitoris itself.

## Anaesthesia, duration and hospital stay

Labiaplasty can be done under **local anaesthesia with sedation**, or under **spinal or general anaesthesia**. The choice depends on how much is being done and what you are comfortable with, and the anaesthetist will discuss it with you.

The operation takes about **45 minutes to an hour**.

It is a **day-care procedure**. Most women go home the same day, a few hours after surgery. Someone should accompany you home.

## Recovery

**Day 1.** The area is swollen, bruised and sore, often more than women expect from so small an operation. This is normal. Cold compresses, regular painkillers, loose cotton clothing and rest make a real difference. Passing urine may sting a little; pouring plain water over the area while you do so helps.

**The first week.** Swelling is at its worst in the first few days and then begins to ease. Keep the area clean and dry, and pat rather than rub. Slight oozing or spotting on a pad is expected. Most women are back at desk work in three to seven days.

**Weeks 2 to 4.** The stitches dissolve, which can cause itching. Walking and everyday activity are fine. Avoid cycling, the gym, swimming, tampons and intercourse for now, and wear loose clothes.

**By six weeks.** Most women are healed. After a check-up, you can return to exercise and intercourse. Some swelling lingers, and the final shape settles over the following few months, so please do not judge the result in the first weeks.

Call us sooner if you have heavy bleeding, a rapidly growing painful swelling, fever, foul-smelling discharge, or a wound that seems to be opening.

## Risks and complications

Labiaplasty is a small operation, but it is still surgery on a sensitive area, and you should know what can go wrong.

- **Bleeding or a blood collection (haematoma)** under the skin. Small ones settle on their own; a larger one occasionally needs to be drained.
- **Infection**, uncommon and treated with antibiotics.
- **Wound separation.** Part of the stitch line may open, especially if the area is strained early. It usually heals with dressings, and occasionally needs a small repair later.
- **Scarring**, which usually fades but can sometimes stay thick or tender.
- **Asymmetry.** No two sides are ever perfectly identical, before or after surgery.
- **Too little or too much removed.** Removing too much cannot be undone, which is why we plan conservatively.
- **Changes in sensation**, either numbness or increased sensitivity. These usually settle but can occasionally persist.
- **Discomfort during intercourse**, usually temporary while the scar softens.
- **Dissatisfaction with the appearance**, and occasionally the need for a small revision.
- **Anaesthetic risks**, which the anaesthetist will explain separately.

> **"Will it look completely natural afterwards?"**
> In most women, once healing is complete, the result looks natural and the scars are hard to find. But I will not promise perfection, because healing is individual and no surgeon controls it fully. What I can do is plan carefully, remove only what needs removing, and tell you before the operation what is realistic for your own anatomy.

## Alternatives

**No treatment at all.** If the examination shows that your labia are within the usual range and cause no physical trouble, leaving them alone is a perfectly sound decision.

**Simple measures.** Looser, breathable clothing, a plain moisturising barrier cream, avoiding harsh soaps, and a better cycle seat can settle rubbing and irritation in many women.

**Treating an underlying problem.** Itching, soreness or swelling is sometimes due to an infection or a skin condition of the vulva, which needs medical treatment, not surgery.

**Counselling.** When the distress is mainly about appearance, particularly after comparing with images online, talking it through with a doctor or counsellor often helps more than an operation would.

Laser treatments do not reduce the size of the labia. ## Preparing for your surgery

- Plan the date so that it does not fall during your period.
- Tell us about allergies, diabetes, thyroid problems, blood thinners, any tendency to bleed or scar heavily, and previous operations.
- If you smoke or use tobacco, stop for a few weeks before and after, because it slows wound healing.
- Do not wax, shave or use hair-removal cream on the area in the days before, unless we have asked you to.
- Do not eat or drink for the number of hours the anaesthetist advises, usually six to eight.
- Keep loose cotton underwear, soft sanitary pads and a cold pack ready at home.
- Arrange for someone to take you home, and keep a few days free of work and household strain.
- Write down your questions and bring them. There is no question too small or too embarrassing.
