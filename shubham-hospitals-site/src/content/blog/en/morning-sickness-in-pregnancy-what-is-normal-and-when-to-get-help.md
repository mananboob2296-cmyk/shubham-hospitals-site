Somewhere around the sixth week, a lot of women sit across from me and say the same thing, half laughing and half close to tears: "Everyone told me this would be a little morning sickness. Nobody told me it would last all day."

They are right. The name is misleading. Pregnancy nausea can arrive at breakfast, at lunch, or when someone opens the tiffin at your desk. It is very common, it is usually harmless, and it is also sometimes more than you should be putting up with. Let me help you tell the difference.

## Why it happens

Nobody has one neat explanation, but the rise in pregnancy hormones in the first trimester is thought to play a big part, along with a heightened sense of smell and a stomach that empties more slowly. It tends to be worse when you are tired, hungry or dehydrated, which is why a bad morning can spiral.

What I want you to take from this is that it is not your fault. It is not caused by something you ate, and it is not a sign that you are "weak" or coping badly.

## What is usually normal

Mild to moderate nausea, with or without an occasional vomit, is the everyday version. If you can still eat something, keep most fluids down, pass urine regularly and get through your day, however grumpily, you are almost certainly in this group.

Here is a quick self-check. Most of these are reassuring if they describe you:

- You vomit once or twice a day at most, and can drink afterwards
- You are passing urine several times a day, and it is a light colour
- You feel better after a small snack or a rest
- Your weight is holding roughly steady

## Things that genuinely help

None of these is magic, but together they take the edge off.

**Eat small and often.** An empty stomach makes nausea worse. Keep dry biscuits, murmura, khakhra or a banana by the bed and have a few bites before you stand up in the morning.

**Sip, don't gulp.** Cold water, nimbu pani, coconut water, or thin buttermilk taken in small sips through the day is easier than a full glass at once.

**Follow your nose.** If cooking smells set you off, let someone else cook, eat food that is cold or at room temperature, and keep the kitchen windows open. Plain, bland food is fine for a few weeks.

**Ginger, if you like it.** Many women find ginger tea or a small piece of ginger soothing. It is a comfort measure, not a treatment, so use it as a helper and not as a reason to delay a check-up.

**Rest when you can.** Tiredness makes queasiness worse.

> **"Can I just take the prenatal vitamin tablet on an empty stomach?"**
>
> Iron and some vitamin tablets can make nausea much worse, especially when taken first thing. Try taking yours with food or at bedtime, and tell us if it is still upsetting your stomach. We can often change the timing or the preparation instead of you struggling through it.

## Myth versus fact

**Myth:** "The baby will be starved if I can't eat properly."
**Fact:** In early pregnancy, the baby's needs are small, and a few weeks of poor appetite in an otherwise well woman is not usually harmful. What we worry about is dehydration and rapid weight loss in you, not a missed meal.

**Myth:** "Vomiting means I will lose the pregnancy."
**Fact:** Ordinary pregnancy sickness does not cause miscarriage.

**Myth:** "It's all in the mind."
**Fact:** No. It has a physical basis, and severe forms are a real medical condition.

## Signs that mean you should come in, not wait

Some women develop a severe form of pregnancy sickness, sometimes called hyperemesis. It is not about being dramatic or sensitive. Please contact us the same day if you have any of these:

- You cannot keep down fluids, or food, for a day
- You are vomiting many times a day
- You are passing very little urine, or it is dark
- You feel dizzy or faint when you stand
- You are losing weight, or your clothes suddenly feel loose
- There is blood in your vomit
- You have severe stomach pain, a severe headache, or fever

> **"I'm embarrassed to call. Everybody has morning sickness."**
>
> Please do call. I would much rather see someone early, give her a drip and the right medicine, and send her home feeling human again than have her arrive three days later, very dry and exhausted. It is a treatable condition and you are not troubling us.

## What to expect at your appointment

We start by listening: how many weeks you are, how often you are vomiting, what you can keep down, and whether you are passing urine. We check your pulse, blood pressure and weight, and look for signs of dehydration.

We may arrange a scan, since nausea can be stronger when there is more than one baby, and to confirm that the pregnancy is settling as it should. Our guide to [pregnancy scans and when each one is done](/blogs/pregnancy-scans-explained-which-scan-when-why-in-amravati/) explains the schedule. Depending on how you are, we may suggest blood tests, fluids through a drip, and medicines that are used safely in pregnancy. Most women feel much better within a day of getting fluids and the right treatment.
